# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Single operator (Faizan) managing 9+ Facebook pages. Works from a dim room at night or a bright
office during the day. Power user — not a novice. The tool is a means to an end: get posts
scheduled and published fast, then close the tab.

## Product Purpose

Self-hosted social media scheduling for Facebook Pages and Instagram Business accounts.
Replace Hootbuffer/Buffer with a local, file-based tool that the operator controls completely.
Success is: zero missed schedules, clear status at a glance, minimal friction between "I have
content" and "it's scheduled."

## Brand Personality

"The Publishing Desk": Sleek, industrial, sharp. The tool feels like a high-end piece of hardware. Chrome dissolves, posts remain. Think professional audio equipment or a modern newsroom: high information density, high-contrast typography, no decorative flourishes, every pixel earns its place.

Voice: direct, concise, professional. "Published" not "Your post is live!" "Failed" not "Something went wrong."

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS cream**: No pastel gradients, round icons, hero-metric templates, or
  illustration-heavy empty states. No "modern SaaS" clichés.
- **Generic AI-generated UI**: No glassmorphism, no gradient-on-text, no floating blur panels.
  Hand-crafted feel, not template.
- **Hootbuffer/Buffer**: Feature-bloated, noisy, slow. Meta Lab is the opposite — sparse,
  fast, focused.

## Design Principles

1. **Content is the hero.** The post preview, its status, and its schedule own the interface.
   Navigation, filters, and chrome are subordinate.

2. **Status must be instant.** Scheduled / published / failed — difference visible from
   peripheral vision via color + shape, not by reading text.

3. **One click per action.** Minimize confirmation dialogs, multi-step forms, and modals.
   The operator knows what they're doing.

4. **Sparse is fast.** Empty space is a performance claim. Fewer cards, tighter tables, less
   visual noise. Every component must justify its existence.

5. **Dark-first, light-capable.** Primary use is at night. Light mode must exist but is
   secondary — no compromise on dark quality.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA contrast on all text. Status colors distinguishable without relying on hue alone
  (shape + position also signal status).
- Reduced motion respected. Animations are optional enhancements, not required for understanding.
- Single-operator: no multi-user or team considerations.
